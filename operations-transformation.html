FEATURED CASE STUDY · OMNICHANNEL BUSINESS BUILDING

Building a Home Try-On Business and Transforming Its Operating Model

Lenskart · Project Head – Home Try-On · April 2014 – September 2015
2014–2015 · 7 Cities

Business Context: Lenskart was developing an assisted home eye-testing and eyewear sales model. The challenge was to build a scalable operating model that could deliver customer appointments, professional eye testing and product sales directly at customers' homes.

Mandate & Responsibilities: I led the team and was responsible for the conceptualisation and execution of the overall initiative. My mandate covered: conceptualisation and operating strategy, team leadership and hiring, deployment planning, operating kit design and distribution, workforce model, commercial and compensation structure, field operations, appointment fulfilment, and operational performance and business outcomes.

Figure 1: Illustrative in-home optical consultation setup and conceptual model comparison from salaried kits to an entrepreneur-led model.

Stage 1 — Building the Business from the Ground Up

The initial priority was establishing disciplined home appointments, professional clinical testing, and customer trust. The salaried operating foundation comprised:

  • Two-Person Operating Kits: Recruited and deployed paired operating units consisting of two people per kit: one salaried optometrist (carrying a clinical eye-testing retinoscope/autorefractor setup) and one salaried sales executive (managing trial frame inventory).
  • Seven Cities Deployed: Rapidly rolled out operations across seven major metropolitan hubs: Bengaluru, Pune, Chennai, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, and Delhi NCR.
  • Scale Execution: Scaled the operation to 200 fully equipped operating kits across all seven cities, deploying approximately 400 field personnel.
  • Standardised Fulfilment & Return Control: Standardised home appointments, professional eye testing, product selection, and sales fulfilment, while establishing operational controls that brought return percentages under control.

Stage 2 — Pivoting to an Entrepreneur-Led Model

Once the operating model and service reliability were stabilized across all seven cities, the model evolved into an asset-light entrepreneur-led optometrist structure:

  • Independent Entrepreneurship: Optometrists moved towards independent entrepreneurship, operating using their own vehicles rather than centralized company logistics.
  • Combined Role Design: Combined clinical eye testing and consultative eyewear sales into a single role, rather than requiring two salaried people for every operating kit.
  • Minimum Guarantee & Commission: The compensation model included a minimum monthly guarantee, with sales commissions payable only after earnings crossed the minimum monthly guarantee, aligning workforce incentives with performance.
  • Scale & Profitability: The model scaled to an average daily volume of more than 500 appointments (rather than an isolated peak or target), achieving a positive contribution margin and overall profitability.
Operating Integrity Note

The entrepreneur-led model was deliberately introduced only after the 200-kit salaried operation was rigorously established and customer return rates were brought under control. This disciplined sequence ensured clinical quality and brand trust were preserved throughout commercial restructuring.

OPERATIONAL FOUNDATION · 5-YEAR LEADERSHIP JOURNEY

Building the Operational Foundation of Organised Radio Cabs in India

Meru Cabs · Role Progression: Manager – Quality to Head of Operations, Delhi · Nov 2007 – Oct 2012
2007–2012 · Delhi NCR & Pan-India

Five-Year Operational Leadership Journey: I joined Meru Cabs in November 2007 as Manager – Quality, initially responsible for designing organizational policies, operational workflows, and service quality processes. Once the foundational systems and processes stabilized, I gradually transitioned into operations leadership, ultimately leading city operations as Head of Operations, Delhi.

Workforce & Operating Responsibility: Headed the teams managing an operating organisation of approximately 1,500 employees and an active driver partner ecosystem of approximately 2,500–3,000 drivers across Delhi NCR. This involved comprehensive operational coordination across quality assurance, 24×7 dispatch control, driver management, fleet support, and field operations (driver partners operated within the business model, not as direct employees).

A. Pan-India Process & Policy Transformation (KPMG Engagement)

As Meru expanded its national operations, executive management engaged KPMG to formalise enterprise-wide operating policies. I served as Meru's Pan-India Single Point of Contact (SPOC) for the entire KPMG engagement:

  • Served as the central operational bridge and coordinator between KPMG consultants and internal department heads across all functional units (fleet, customer service, technology, finance, and human resources).
  • Gathered existing processes, reviewed and critically challenged proposed recommendations against frontline field realities, and refined policies to ensure they were practical to implement.
  • Supported enterprise-wide implementation and coordinated policy refinement as business requirements and operating conditions evolved.
Scope & Attribution Precision

I served as Meru's SPOC coordinating this extensive work. The policy transformation was a collaborative exercise involving internal department heads and KPMG consultants; it reflects joint governance and coordination rather than sole authorship of organizational policies.

B. Driver Subscription & Entrepreneurial Ecosystem

Meru developed an operating model anchored in a daily driver subscription, establishing an entrepreneurial relationship with driver partners:

  • The operating ecosystem covered approximately 2,000–3,000 drivers in Delhi NCR only.
  • Drivers paid a fixed ₹1,200 per day as a daily subscription fee for the vehicle and automated dispatch business opportunity.
  • Meru's collections teams collected this subscription amount at designated locations across Delhi NCR.
  • Meru provided trip demand and customer bookings through an automated dispatch model.
  • Drivers were responsible for their own fuel expenses and retained all earnings above the daily ₹1,200 subscription amount.
  • Meru's operational teams managed the surrounding ecosystem: dispatch infrastructure, maintenance support, and driver compliance.

C. Airport Business Model Transformation (Prepaid to Postpaid)

Original Model: Arriving passengers originally booked prepaid journeys at designated airport counters. Meru had obtained the airport counter through a formal RFP process and operated under a revenue-share agreement with the airport operator (I did not independently obtain the counter or originate the commercial airport agreement).

Transformed Model: Led the operational implementation and rollout of the transition from prepaid counter bookings to direct curbside boarding and postpaid metered journeys:

  • Direct Curbside Boarding: Passengers walked directly to the curb to board taxis without queueing at terminal counters, with journeys metered and paid at the destination.
  • Operational Rollout: I led the operational implementation and coordination of the new arrangements, managing queue marshals, dispatch lanes, and ground staging protocols.
  • Throughput Impact: Terminal counter queues were eliminated, vehicle turnaround time was reduced by approximately 3×, and airport pickup throughput substantially increased as passenger pickups started earlier.
