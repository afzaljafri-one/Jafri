FEATURED CASE STUDY · OMNICHANNEL BUSINESS BUILDING

Building a Home Try-On Business and Transforming Its Operating Model

Lenskart · Project Head – Home Try-On · April 2014 – September 2015
2014–2015 · 7 Cities

At Lenskart, the business challenge was to take eyewear discovery beyond pure e-commerce by solving the fundamental barrier to online optical purchases: physical fit, prescription verification, and tactile frame evaluation. As Project Head for Home Try-On, I conceived, built, and scaled the operating model from inception, navigating a deliberate two-stage strategic journey.

Figure 1: In-home optical consultation setup and strategic evolution from salaried kits to an entrepreneur-led model.

Stage 1 — Building the Business from the Ground Up

The initial priority was establishing uncompromising operational discipline, clinical standards, and customer trust. The operational foundation comprised:

  • Two-Person Operating Kits: Recruited and trained paired teams consisting of one certified, salaried optometrist (carrying a clinical eye-testing retinoscope/autorefractor setup) and one sales executive (managing trial frame inventory).
  • Multi-City Footprint: Rapidly rolled out operations across seven major metropolitan hubs: Bengaluru, Pune, Chennai, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, and Delhi NCR.
  • Scale Execution: Expanded field deployment from 30 initial team members to 200 fully equipped operating kits, deploying approximately 400 field personnel.
  • Rigorous Return Control: Built scheduling, routing, and optical measurement controls that systematically brought customer return percentages under control, establishing baseline operational stability.

Stage 2 — Pivoting to an Entrepreneur-Led Model

Once the operating model and service reliability were stabilized across all seven cities, the high fixed-overhead structure of salaried, two-person teams presented a ceiling on unit-level profitability. I redesigned the commercial and operational economics:

  • Combined Role Design: Cross-trained and upskilled optometrists to combine clinical eye examination with consultative frame sales, eliminating the need for a separate sales passenger.
  • Asset-Light Fleet Economics: Shifted vehicle ownership and daily route travel responsibility to the optometrists, dramatically cutting centralized capital and logistical overhead.
  • Performance-Linked Compensation: Restructured compensation from a fixed high salary to a lower fixed base paired with substantial, uncapped sales-linked commissions, giving optometrists direct entrepreneurial agency in their earning potential.
  • Volume Expansion: The resulting agility and improved unit economics powered the business to scale past 500+ daily Home Try-On appointments while achieving sustainable unit profitability.
Operating Integrity Note

The entrepreneur-led model was deliberately introduced only after the 200-kit salaried operation was rigorously established and customer return rates were brought under control. This disciplined sequence ensured clinical quality and brand trust were never compromised during commercial restructuring.

Building the Operational Foundation of Organised Radio Cabs in India

Meru Cabs · Senior Manager – Operations / Head of Operations, Delhi · Nov 2007 – Oct 2012
2007–2012 · Delhi & Pan-India

During the nascent era of organised metered radio cabs in India, Meru pioneered technology-driven dispatch and fleet operations. As Head of Operations for Delhi and a member of the early operating leadership, my mandate was to build robust city operations, establish 24×7 dispatch reliability, manage critical transportation hubs, and oversee an operating and frontline workforce of approximately 1,500 personnel.

A. Pan-India Process & Policy Transformation (KPMG Engagement)

As Meru's national footprint expanded, the executive management engaged KPMG to formalise enterprise operating policies. I served as Meru's Pan-India Single Point of Contact (SPOC) for the entire KPMG engagement:

  • Served as the central operational bridge between KPMG's advisory team and Meru's internal leadership across fleet, finance, technology, HR, and city general managers.
  • Critically evaluated proposed policies against frontline field realities—ensuring dispatch protocols, driver disciplinary rules, and fleet turnaround procedures were practical, enforceable, and commercially viable.
  • Continuously refined and iterated policy frameworks as market dynamics, regulatory conditions, and fleet density evolved across major Indian hubs.
Scope & Attribution Precision

I served as Meru's SPOC coordinating this extensive work. The policy transformation was a collaborative institutional exercise involving internal business heads and KPMG consultants; it reflects joint governance rather than sole authorship.

B. Driver Subscription & Entrepreneurial Ecosystem

Rather than treating drivers as traditional salaried employees, Meru developed an operating model anchored in a daily subscription fee, fostering an entrepreneurial mindset among driver partners:

  • Managed the day-to-day subscription operating ecosystem covering approximately 2,000–5,000 drivers across multi-city deployments.
  • Drivers paid a predictable daily subscription for their vehicle and tech dispatch access, keeping all surplus trip revenue they generated, which incentivized proactive shift availability and passenger service.
  • Established daily collection routines, maintenance tracking, and behavioral training to sustain driver retention and fleet asset longevity across Delhi NCR.

C. Airport Business Model Transformation (Prepaid to Postpaid)

Airport terminals represented Meru's highest-density passenger touchpoint, but the historical prepaid counter model suffered from severe operational bottlenecks: long passenger queues at payment counters, manual cash handling, fixed zone fare disputes, and sluggish vehicle curb turnover.

I led the commercial redesign and operational implementation of the transition from the prepaid airport model to a streamlined postpaid model:

  • Passenger Throughput: Eliminated terminal ticketing counter queues by enabling arriving travelers to walk directly to the curb and board waiting cabs immediately.
  • Digital Metering: Integrated automated trip start telemetry at airport departure barriers with fare settlement occurring at the trip's destination.
  • Fleet Velocity: Accelerated vehicle curb turnaround time by more than 3×, reducing terminal congestion, increasing driver trip completion rates, and maximizing peak-hour revenue.
BUSINESS TURNAROUND · COMMERCIAL & CASH FLOW OPTIMISATION

Operational Turnaround, P&L Discipline & Asset-Light Restructuring

Meru Cabs · Deputy General Manager – North / Business Head · Feb 2017 – Mar 2018
2017–2018 · North India Region

Context of Return & Market Dynamics

In February 2017, Meru's board rehired me in recognition of my earlier contributions to building the company's foundational operations. The company was actively preparing for a potential new funding round and sought to reinforce investor confidence by bringing back members of its original leadership team who possessed deep domain knowledge of Meru's operating fabric.

The ambition was to rebuild Meru's market standing toward the operational stature of its peak 2009 period. This initiative occurred during intense market uncertainty: by November 2017, industry speculation mounted regarding potential Ola–Uber consolidation driven by SoftBank's concurrent investments in both ride-hailing giants. Institutional investors were actively evaluating whether to back a capitalized third player rather than simply contesting the second position in a subsidised duopoly.

Attribution & Historical Context

My return did not secure the funding round, nor do I claim that the round was successful or that I single-handedly restored Meru to its 2009 peak stature. Rather, my leadership was focused on the concrete operational, commercial, and financial restructuring executed across the North India business during this tenure.

Operational & Financial Turnaround Deliverables

Taking P&L ownership as Business Head for North India, I spearheaded rigorous operational turnarounds across airport services, corporate mobility, and fleet maintenance:

  • Airport Consumer Revenue Expansion: Re-engineered airport curb dispatch protocols, passenger queuing, and counter presence at Indira Gandhi International Airport (DIAL), expanding airport consumer revenue from ₹29 lakh/month to ₹42 lakh/month.
  • Corporate Mobility Scaling: Restructured B2B enterprise mobility contracts and service delivery across NCR, growing corporate mobility contract revenue from ₹5 lakh/month to ₹18 lakh/month.
  • Fleet Maintenance Overhaul: Instituted strict preventative maintenance oversight, rationalised parts inventory, and audited vendor contracts, cutting regional fleet repair and maintenance (R&M) costs by 70%.
  • Cash Flow Breakeven: Achieved sustained positive operating cash flow within 10 months through cost containment, tighter receivables collection, and asset-light fleet transition.
